Estate Planning & Probate

Every document, every beneficiary, in order.

Publius helps estate and probate attorneys organize complex estates, decedent records, and trust administration so families receive accurate, timely, and well-documented service.

Capabilities

Estate document review

Indexes wills, trusts, codicils, and amendments across decades of records — flagging conflicting provisions, superseded clauses, and missing signatures so nothing is overlooked during administration.

Asset inventories

Pulls asset details from account statements, deeds, titles, and tax returns into a single inventory with sourced citations — surfacing discrepancies between reported values and supporting records.

Beneficiary communications

Organizes all beneficiary correspondence, notices, and distribution records in one searchable workspace — so every communication is documented and defensible.

Outcomes

What this means in practice.

  • Faster estate administration timelines
  • Reduced risk of missed assets or beneficiaries
  • More transparent, defensible records of work

Common Questions

Frequently asked

Can Publius help organize a complex estate with decades of documents?

Yes. Publius indexes wills, trusts, codicils, amendments, deeds, and financial records across decades of estate history. It flags conflicting provisions, superseded clauses, and version discrepancies so attorneys can identify issues early — before they become disputes during administration.

How does Publius handle trust administration records?

Publius organizes trust instruments, amendments, distribution records, and beneficiary correspondence into a single workspace. Attorneys can trace any distribution back to its governing provision and supporting documentation — building a clear, defensible record of fiduciary compliance.

Can Publius track probate deadlines and court filings?

Publius connects court filings, inventory requirements, and notice obligations into a structured timeline. Attorneys can see which deadlines are approaching, which filings are outstanding, and which beneficiaries have been notified — reducing the risk of missed steps in the probate process.
